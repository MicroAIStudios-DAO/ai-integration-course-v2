No marketing copy has been rewritten. Per the work plan, this memo presents

## What actually ships today (ground truth)

- **7 modules, 52 lessons.** M1 "Foundations of AI Integration" (free
  lessons), then AI in Finance & Investment, AI Entrepreneurship & Startups,
  AI for Small Business, AI for Real Estate, AI for Executive Leadership,
  AI and Creative Industries — an **applied/business AI curriculum**, not a
  systems-engineering program.
- Each module also carries a hands-on **project lesson** (e.g. "Small
  Business AI Helpdesk", "Real Estate Deal Underwriter") and there are five
  technical **Founders lessons** (Content Architect, Informed Architect,
  Persistent Memory/Vector DBs, Governed Agents, Deployment Pipeline).
- Real technical infrastructure exists: the Allie tutor, the Forge sandbox,
  Pinecone/MCP/Governance lab routes.
- **5 of 52 lessons have a video.** (The placeholder box is now removed.)

## The three-way contradiction (what each surface sells)

| Surface | Story |
|---|---|
| Homepage | Advanced systems engineering: fault-tolerant agents, Serper.dev, Pinecone, 4 engineering modules ("Module 01–04"), "we reject the prompt engineering gimmick" |
| /pricing & /start-trial | Non-technical quick wins: "even if you're not technical", copy-paste starter scripts |

## Option A — "Practical AI for Operators" (align copy to what ships)

**Audience:** business owners, operators, and executives (SMB, real estate,
executive leadership, creative) who want working automations without an
engineering team; developers welcome via the Founders track.

**Story:** implementation-first AI training that ends every module in a
deployable project, with an AI tutor and sandbox so non-engineers actually
ship. The homepage is rewritten around the real 7 modules and projects;
pricing's accessible tone stays; the Founders lessons become the advertised
"builder track" for technical users.

**Why choose it:**
- Honest on day one with zero curriculum work — the product already delivers
  this promise, and the trial experience matches the ad.
- Coheres with the library/solutions pages (RAG for small business, real
  estate/e-commerce/law) and with /pricing — two of three surfaces already
  sell this.
- Fastest path to conversion lift: the funnel stops arguing with itself.

**Costs / risks:**
- Crowded "AI for business" category; the homepage's distinctive
  engineering-school brand is retired.
- The technical blog posts (your only link-worthy SEO assets) attract
  developers who then meet a business curriculum — mitigated by the Founders
  track framing, not eliminated.

## Option B — "Systems-First AI Engineering School" (build to the homepage's promise)

**Audience:** developers and technical founders shipping production agents.

**Story:** keep the homepage's engineering-school positioning and make it
true: the five Founders lessons + module projects + Forge/labs become the
spine of a real Modules 01–04 engineering curriculum; the seven business
modules are reframed as "applied industry tracks" (electives), not the core.

**Why choose it:**
- Genuinely differentiated vs the AI-literacy crowd; justifies premium
  pricing and the certification framing.
- Matches the technical blog content that drives your SEO/AEO, and the
  infrastructure you already built (Forge, labs, tutor).

**Costs / risks:**
- **Significant content build**: the promised engineering modules mostly do
  not exist as advertised. Until they do, every trial that starts from the
  homepage story churns on contact with the curriculum — this is the current
  state, and it is the biggest conversion leak.
- Pricing/start-trial copy must move up-market, which shrinks the addressable
  cold audience.

## Recommendation

**Option A now.** It is the only option that is honest this quarter, it
requires copy work not content work, and it directly closes the leak (trial
reality matching trial promise). Option B remains available as a deliberate
brand migration once the engineering modules actually exist — the Founders
track keeps that door open and gives you signal on technical demand.
